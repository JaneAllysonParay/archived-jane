01 / TEAM CAPSTONE

DISTYNC: Connecting people, records, and relief operations.

A multi-office disaster relief management system designed to make fragmented data and workflows easier to monitor—while respecting established LGU procedures.

01 / CONTEXT

The challenge

Disaster-relief operations involve coordinated work across barangays, the MSWDO, and the Office of the Mayor. Paper forms, spreadsheets, and separate records made household monitoring, distribution tracking, and inventory reconciliation harder to consolidate.

Our team developed DISTYNC, a centralized role-based Progressive Web Application (PWA) that connects those workflows with reporting, descriptive analytics, and selected offline support.

03 / BUSINESS ANALYSIS

How the operations connect

The MSWDO creates the disaster-event context, barangay officials register households and verify relief claims, and DISTYNC records accepted distributions and their inventory effects. The Mayor's Office maintains stock and relief-pack templates in parallel. Successful records and synchronization support monitoring.

04 / DATA ANALYTICS

Turning records into insight

Descriptive analytics: Barangay operational heat map shows aggregated operational indicators—not household locations or hazard predictions.

Forecasting: Python analytics service implements Moving Average, Exponential Smoothing and Trend Projection. Where suitable history exists, rolling-origin evaluation compares errors using MAE and RMSE. Model recommendations are conditional, not claims of field accuracy.

05 / QUALITY ASSURANCE

Tested, evaluated, and improved

Our manuscript reports the following results under controlled disaster-response scenarios, not production-service guarantees.

22 / 22PWA test cases
23 / 23Security test cases
72 / 72Accepted UAT cases

Across 15 participants, 72 UAT cases were accepted. The reported System Usability Scale mean was 67.17, just below the benchmark of 68.

Passing defined test cases does not mean the system is fully secure or already field-deployed for live disaster operations.
06 / REFLECTION

What I would improve next

Before wider adoption, DISTYNC needs continued usability refinement, real-world field validation, stronger protection for sensitive locally stored data, and tested backup and recovery practices.
